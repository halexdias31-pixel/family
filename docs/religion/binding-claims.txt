O1 (P6) — RESOLVED: P6 holds, with one stated caveat.
 (a) Jerusalem 1672 (Confession of Dositheus) Decree 2, Robertson 1899 tr.: "We believe the Divine and Sacred Scriptures
     to be God-taught; and, therefore, we ought to believe the same without doubting; yet not otherwise than as the
     Catholic Church hath interpreted and delivered." It goes on to defend the Church's interpretation against Calvinists
     and calls the Church unable to err. Its subject is WHO interprets Scripture, not historical accuracy of each narrative;
     it says nothing about historical detail. Standing: a local synod; widely received in the Greek Church (Schaff-Herzog
     calls its sanction "universal" among Greeks) but it is not an ecumenical council. Ware (The Orthodox Church) treats the
     17th-c. confessions as below the Seven Councils and notes Latin influence (secondary snippets; full-text check pending).
 (b) No Ecumenical Council (I-VII) and no clause of the Creed defines inerrancy of historical detail; the Creed binds
     "spoke by the prophets" (inspiration) and the dated anchor "under Pontius Pilate".
 (c) Trullo canon 2 lists the canons/fathers to be received; it fixes sources, not inerrancy.
 Owner's position ("local synods not binding on all Orthodox") is broadly CONFIRMED as stated by mainstream Orthodox writers,
 but even if Dositheus were fully binding, Decree 2 does not assert detail-level historicity, so P6 survives either way.
 Caveat: a reader may infer detail-inerrancy from "God-taught ... without doubting". That is an inference, not the text.

O2 (P3) — RESOLVED for death ON THE CROSS.
 (i) No recognised Sunni scholar found who affirms Jesus died on the cross. Those who do (Ayoub; Ismaili writers; Ahmadis
     only partly) are outside Sunnism.
 (ii) Yes, death without crucifixion: Mahmud Shaltut (Shaykh al-Azhar 1958-63), al-Fatawa: tawaffa = death; "God caused him
     to die at the end of his appointed term and elevated him"; "his enemies could not kill him or crucify him"; no
     Qur'an/Sunna proof for bodily ascension/return. Rashid Rida (al-Manar) read tawaffa as death (secondary, Ahmadi-sourced
     quotes). Ghamidi (modern Pakistani) also departs from the alive-in-heaven view (not fetched). All still deny the
     crucifixion -> contradicted by P1 -> thesis holds.
 (iii) Status: classical ijma' claimed for raised alive (Ibn Atiyya; Ibn Hajar: reports mutawatir). Shaltut's dissent is a
     minority view (he remained Shaykh al-Azhar), not treated as leaving Islam. Denial of the
     cross-death is the verse's plain text, so no Sunni reading permits it.
