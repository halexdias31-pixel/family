## The keypad is the only keyboard: every answer box is the pad's, `abc` turns the pad to letters, and the phone's keyboard has no way in

**The owner, 8 Oct:**

> *"Make the keypad never need to use their own keyboard the key pad seems to allow ios keyboard to show I just want self contained system really"*

That reverses a decision written at the top of `js/keypad.js` since the pad was built (266): *"worded answer normal device keyboard."* The pad was for maths; words, and any answer the pad had no key for, went to the phone's keyboard on purpose.

### Every route the phone's keyboard had, measured before anything changed

Read-only audit with real CDP touch in Chromium at 390x844 (iPhone) and 820x1180 (iPad), and WebKit's own source for what iOS does (iOS itself cannot run here).

| Route | What it was | Counted |
|---|---|---|
| The worded answer box | `ansBox_` drew a plain `<textarea>`, no `inputmode`, no `readonly` | 2,183 of 8,006 cards |
| A practical's worksheet | `guideBox_` drew three plain textareas, spellcheck on | 82 practicals × 3 = 246 |
| `abc` on the maths pad | `kpWords_` set `inputmode="text"`, then `blur()` and `focus()` inside the tap — the keyboard up on purpose | every maths box; 532 of 5,149 could not be answered without it (`3n`, `y = …`, `2:3`, `x < 3`) |
| An iPad's hardware keyboard | WebKit's `_requiresKeyboardWhenFirstResponder` and the hardware branch of `_elementDidFocus` ignore `inputmode`; they refuse only a `readonly` field. Measured: physical keys typed straight into the maths box past its slots (`7cmx2`) | every box |
| Apple Pencil Scribble | `_focusTextInputContext` refuses only `readonly` | every box |
| Long press / second tap | the invisible input lay over the drawing and took the touch: Paste, AutoFill, Scan Text (`captureTextFromCamera` refused only for `readonly`) | every box |
| iOS before 12.2 | ignores `inputmode` entirely | every box |

Not routes: multiple-choice options (buttons), the pen page (no text field; its "words" are spans you tap to ring), a redraw (`kpLive_` always rebuilt the box from `kpField_`), and sign-in (nothing on a question page opens it). The Find search box and the sign-in email and PIN fields use the phone's keyboard by design and are not answer boxes; they were left alone.

### The decision: a real field, locked in its markup, that the finger never touches

Three shapes were weighed.

- **(a) The pad's box stays a real `<input>`/`<textarea>`, with `readonly` AND `inputmode="none"` written in the markup.** Built. `readonly` is what closes the routes `inputmode` leaves open — every WebKit path that ignores `inputmode` refuses a readonly field, and it works on every iOS version. `inputmode="none"` stays as the second lock. `readonly` stops the BROWSER writing into the box and nothing else: `.value`, `setSelectionRange`, `selectionStart` and a dispatched `input` all still work (measured), so the save path (`ansStore_`), `qp-check`, Mark with AI, `ansRefresh_`, `kpLive_` and the ~40 places the checks write an answer are all unchanged.
- **(b) A non-editable `role="textbox"` with the value in a data attribute.** Marginally stronger (an element with no type starts no input session at all) and the same keyboard outcome, at the cost of rewriting every reader of `.value` and reimplementing the selection API. Kept as the fallback if a look on the iPhone ever shows the ⌃⌄ Done bar over a readonly box — the readers to change are listed in the investigation.
- **(c) `contenteditable`.** Rejected: WebKit reports a contenteditable element as `isReadOnly = false` always, so iOS treats it as an editable field whatever the page does.

**The finger never touches the box.** It is `pointer-events: none` under its own drawing (`.kp-show`, unselectable, no callout), so a tap lands on the drawing inside the `<label>` that wraps both. The label's own click is cancelled — it would hand a second click to the box and focus it natively — and keypad.js focuses the box itself and puts the caret where the finger was (`kpHit_`). Its `mousedown` is swallowed too, the pad's own trick: without it, the prototype measured a tap on character 18 landing at 316, because the tap blurred the box, the pad closed, and the refocus moved the caret to the end and lifted the card before the tap was read.

### What changed

- **One box, drawn in one place.** `kpField_(k, val, kind, signs, name)` draws both kinds: a maths `<input>` and a worded `<textarea>` (an `<input>` strips newlines, and answers typed on phones before today may hold them), each `.qp-ans-in.kp-in` with `data-kp="maths|words"`, `readonly`, `inputmode="none"`, an `aria-label`, under its drawing. `ansBox_` and `guideBox_` both call it; neither writes a field of its own any more. Nothing in js/ writes `inputmode` or `readonly` by script, and check-flow says so.
- **`abc` turns the pad to letters; `123` turns it back.** `kpWords_`, the `is-words` class and its restore in `focusout` are deleted. A worded box opens on the letters, a maths box on the maths keys, and a box retaken after a redraw keeps the face the child was on.
- **The letters** (`KP_ABC`): the phone's own rows — q to p, a to l set in half a key, ⇧ z to m and the apostrophe, `, .` a space bar `? -` — on sixty columns, so the bottom row is the maths pad's own: `123 ← → ⌫ ✓` in the same places and colours, with ↵ (a new line) where ␣ is, on a worded box. Five rows at the maths keys' heights, so the pad does not change height when it turns over and the card does not move. The gap between faces is inside each key's box (a transparent border), a tap in a gutter or between rows goes to the nearest key in that row (`kpNear_`), and the pressed letter shows above the finger. Narrower than 44px on a phone and it has to be — ten letters need 440px and a phone has 288 inside its gutters — so `check/ui.js` carries the reason (`ACCEPTED_TAP`, `kp-ch`); from 640px wide the letters pad widens and its keys are 60px.
- **Capitals** the phone's way: ⇧ once is one capital, twice inside 350ms is caps lock (a bar under the key, `aria-pressed`), and a worded box puts a capital in by itself at the start, after `. ? !` and a space, and on a new line. Never in a maths box. Only two consecutive ⇧ presses are a double tap — ⇧ a ⇧ typed fast is two capitals.
- **The signs row.** With the phone's keyboard gone, 157 marked questions had no way to type their answer: `<` 78, `≤` 57, `:` 51, `>` 23, `≥` 12. A row of `< ≤ ≥ > : ±` sits above the digits on a box whose scheme uses one — or, with no scheme, whose worked answer LEADS with one, the part before its first dash (`140 < h ≤ 150 — …`, 48 boxes) — and on no other (`kpSigns_`, `data-kp-signs`). On every box it would be a sixth row covering 48px more of the card on the 5,000 that never use it. The first build read the whole worked answer and `check/ui.js` caught it: the prose is the mark scheme's and names wrong forms (1F Q23b: *"the right numbers in the wrong form, eg 5 : 9"*), which gave 411 more boxes the row, and at 320 that sixth row lifted Q23b's card 11px off the top of the screen (PANE OFF THE SCREEN). `markAnswer_` already read `x<=-4` and `2:5`.
- **A worded answer is drawn as text** (`kpDraw_`): escaped, newlines kept, the caret a span, never `typeset_` — which reads `well-known` as a minus and `and/or` as a fraction. `ansRefresh_` (an answer filled from another device) now draws through `kpRender_`; it called `kpTypeset_` on every box. The drawing grows to about five lines and scrolls inside, with the caret's line kept in view by `scrollTop`, never `scrollIntoView`.
- **A laptop's or an iPad's keyboard** goes through the pad's own edit (`kpKeys_`): printable keys, Backspace (which now keeps a fraction's brackets paired, as ⌫ does), Delete, ← →, Home/End, ↑ ↓ by line in words, Enter as ✓, Shift+Enter a new line, Escape to put the pad away; Ctrl/⌘ and Tab are the browser's; Option alone types (`≤` on a Mac). A paste is typed through the pad too. `*` in a maths box is `×`.
- **⌫, ← and → repeat while held** (450ms, then every 70ms); the click at the lift is swallowed.
- **The pad never covers the box as it grows.** After every key and every turn of the pad the room is asked again (`kpKeepClear_`), answered by the lift (`kpLift_`, idempotent), never `kpRoom_`. `kpRoom_` and `kpLift_` measure the pill the child sees (`.qp-ans`), not the invisible box. find.js's auto-grow skips the pad's box: the prototype measured it making the invisible textarea ~600px tall and the lift throwing the card 654px off the screen.
- **A screen reader** hears the box's content a moment after the last key (`#kp-say`, `aria-live`), maths read the way a person says it ("3 over 4").
- **The worksheet's look moved** from the textarea to `.gd-box .kp-field` (the `--sunk` ground, the ruled line, gold on focus), with a gold caret, because the drawing's default caret is the paper's ink and vanishes on black. The unused `.qp-ans-in` and `.qp-bar textarea.qp-ans-in` rules went.
- `enterkeyhint`, `autocorrect`, `spellcheck` are all off on every box. `--css-version` is `2026-10-08-keypad-own`.

**What went, said out loud:** spelling help, autocorrect, predictions, dictation and copy-and-paste by touch on a worded answer. They came with the phone's keyboard and went with it. A laptop still pastes, cuts, undoes and selects with ⇧ (put back after the review, below). **What a laptop still loses:** selecting with the mouse (a drag or a double click on the drawing places the caret and nothing more, because the box under it takes no pointer), the right-click menu's Cut and Paste (the menu is the page's, over a drawing), and Enter for a new line in a worded box — Enter is ✓, as on the pad, and ⇧+Enter is the new line. That last one is a choice, not a loss.

### The review of 8 Oct, and what it changed

Four findings, each checked before it was fixed; all four were real.

- **Eleven marked questions could not be answered right on the pad at all** (two reviewers found it separately). Their schemes hold a sign AND a word — `x < 4 or x > 5`, `93.5 ≤ length < 94.5`, `6:18 pm` — so `ansMaths_` gives them a WORDS box, and the signs row was drawn on maths boxes only (`ansBox_` passed `maths && kpSigns_(x)`, `kpLayout_` said `!words &&`). Neither face had `<`, `≤` or `:`, every typeable alternative (`6.18pm`, `93.5 to 94.5`) is marked wrong, and before this change the phone's keyboard had typed them. The census of 157 counted these and assumed the row fixed them all; it fixed the 146 on maths boxes. Now `ansBox_` marks the box by its scheme whatever its kind and `kpLayout_` draws the row on a worded box's `123` face; the letters stay five rows. 42 worded boxes carry the row (22 with a scheme, 20 from the lead of their worked answer); the eleven are Q-1CM-quadratic-inequalities-2/4/6/8/10, Q-1CM-error-intervals-18/19/20, Q-1CM-hcf-and-lcm-13/15 and Q-CBM-time-10. By real touch at 390, `x<4 or x>5`, `6:18` and `93.5≤length<94.5` were each typed across both faces and marked Correct, the pill 12–79px clear of the six-row pad.
- **A laptop lost what `readonly` took from a text box**, measured in Chromium: ⇧+← moved the caret and dropped the selection; Ctrl/⌘+Z did nothing, so Ctrl+A and a letter wiped a paragraph for good; Ctrl/⌘+X did nothing; AltGr characters (`€`, `á`, and `@ { }` on many European layouts) were dropped, because AltGr arrives as Ctrl+Alt and Ctrl meant "the browser's". Put back in `kpKeys_`: ⇧ with ← → Home End (↑ ↓ in words) grows the selection from its anchor; an undo history per box (by `data-k`, so it survives a redraw), a run of letters one step a word at a time, Ctrl/⌘+Z back and ⇧+Ctrl/⌘+Z or Ctrl+Y forward; a cut puts the selection on the clipboard through the `cut` event (Chromium fires it on a readonly box) and takes it out through the pad's edit, with a copy-and-delete where a browser fires none; AltGr, or Ctrl+Alt with a one-character key, types. A maths selection is drawn now too — its two ends typeset into the answer and a band laid between them (`kpSelBand_`), because half a fraction cannot be one `<mark>`. Measured in real Chromium: ⇧←×4 selected `fast` backward, Ctrl+X left `Rate up ` with `fast` on the clipboard, Ctrl+Z and ⇧Ctrl+Z walked it, Ctrl+A `Q` Ctrl+Z gave the paragraph back, CDP AltGr+E typed `€`, ⇧Home on `12` drew a 20×19 band. Left, and said above: the mouse, the right-click menu, Enter.
- **The source scan that guards the lock caught two spellings of the write.** One line added to keypad.js — a double tap calling `toggleAttribute('readonly', false)` and `setAttribute('inputMode', 'text')` — stayed green, because HTML attribute names ignore case and the pattern did not, and it did not know `toggleAttribute`, backticks or a bracket write; the MutationObserver sees only the routes the journey walks. The pattern is now case-insensitive, knows `set/remove/toggleAttribute`, the `NS` forms and `removeNamedItem`, any quote, `.readOnly`/`.inputMode`/`.contentEditable =` and their bracket forms — `contenteditable` because it is the third way a keyboard comes up. And a second rule: outside `kpField_`, no shipped js/ file may write `data-do="qp-ans"` or the `.qp-ans-in` class (a selector that finds one is a read), so a new card that draws its own answer field fails whoever draws it, not only if this journey learns to draw that card.

Mutations, each red and the real files green after: the review's double-tap line, a bracket write, a backtick `removeAttribute`, `contentEditable` on the drawing, a `<textarea class="qp-ans-in" data-do="qp-ans">` in find.js, a `setAttribute('data-do', 'qp-ans')` — six in the lock journey; the signs row maths-only again in `ansBox_`, `!words` back in `kpLayout_`, no undo, every letter its own undo step, Ctrl+Z left to the browser, no AltGr, ⇧ arrows collapsing, a cut that does nothing, a maths selection not drawn — nine in the letters journey.

### Not testable here — one look on a real iPhone and iPad

- **No keyboard and no ⌃⌄ Done bar** on any answer box. WebKit's `requiresAccessoryView` is YES for text fields on an iPhone whatever `readonly` says; UIKit should not show that bar without the keyboard, but the source cannot settle it. If it shows, switch the box to shape (b).
- **An Apple Pencil writing over a box does nothing** (no Scribble palette).
- **A long press shows no edit menu, magnifier or Scan Text.**
- **With a keyboard case on the iPad**, keys type through the pad's rules; iPadOS may show its small hardware-keyboard bar after the first key (it does for any focused non-editable element).
- **VoiceOver's wording** for a readonly box ("read-only"), and the real feel of 28.8px letter cells at 320.
