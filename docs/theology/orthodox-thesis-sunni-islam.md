# Thesis: an Eastern Orthodox case against Sunni Islam

Private study note. Not linked from the site. Each argument is set out as numbered premises with a conclusion. "Valid" means the conclusion follows from the premises; whether it is sound depends on the premises marked *contested*. Sunni positions are stated in their strongest classical form (Ash'ari/Maturidi/Athari creed).

**Thesis.** If Sunni Islam's own commitments are held together, they either contradict each other or imply something Sunni Islam denies. So Sunni Islam is not true as a whole, and the Orthodox confession (one God in three hypostases; Christ crucified and risen) is the better-supported account.

---

## Argument 1: the Scripture dilemma

1. The Qur'an tells the People of the Gospel to judge by what God revealed in it (Q 5:47), and tells the Prophet to ask those who read the Book before him (Q 10:94).
2. The Qur'an says no one can change God's words (Q 6:34, 6:115, 10:64).
3. From (1), the Gospel the Christians held in the 7th century was available and authoritative. A command to judge by a lost or corrupted text would be empty.
4. The Gospel texts held then match, in every relevant claim, the manuscripts we have from well before then (Codex Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, 4th century; papyri such as P46 and P75, c. 200).
5. Those texts teach that Jesus was crucified and died (Mark 15, John 19), and that he is the Son who shares the Father's divine name and worship (John 1:1, 20:28; Phil 2:6–11).
6. The Qur'an denies that Jesus was crucified (Q 4:157) and that he is divine (Q 5:72–75).
7. Then either (a) the 7th-century Gospel was authoritative and uncorrupted, and the Qur'an contradicts a revelation it endorses; or (b) that Gospel was corrupted, and (1) and (2) are false.
8. Either way, a claim the Qur'an makes is false. So the Qur'an is not wholly God's word, which Sunni Islam affirms.

**Form:** a constructive dilemma (7), with modus tollens on Sunni inerrancy (8). It is valid.
**Contested:** (3) and (4). The usual Sunni reply is *tahrif* (corruption) of meaning or text. A corruption of meaning only (*tahrif al-ma'na*) grants that the text is sound, so (5) stands. A corruption of the text must fall after the earliest manuscripts and before 610, and it contradicts (2) and (3).

## Argument 2: the uncreated Qur'an

1. Sunni creed holds that the Qur'an is the uncreated Speech (*kalam*) of God, eternal, and "not He and not other than He" (*la huwa wa la ghayruhu*).
2. So, on Sunni terms, something eternal in God can be distinct from God's essence without being a second god.
3. The Sunni objection to the Trinity (*shirk*) is that an eternal distinct-yet-divine reality in God means more than one god.
4. If (2), the principle behind (3) is false.
5. So the objection in (3) fails by Sunni Islam's own standard.

The Orthodox claim is stronger: the Word (Logos) is eternal, distinct in hypostasis and one in essence (John 1:1). Sunni Islam already accepts the shape of this claim and only denies that the Word is personal.

**Form:** a reductio on premise (3). It is valid.
**Contested:** whether an attribute (*sifa*) and a hypostasis are the same kind of thing. The reply: the objection in (3) was about eternal distinction within God, not about personhood. Once distinction is granted, the remaining question is whether God's Word can be personal. A non-personal Word that speaks, commands and is heard is the less coherent option.

## Argument 3: love and self-sufficiency

1. God is perfect, and so self-sufficient (*al-Ghani*). His perfections do not depend on creation.
2. Sunni Islam names God *al-Wadud*, the Loving, as an eternal perfection.
3. Love in act needs a beloved other than the lover. (Self-love alone is not the perfection meant by *al-Wadud*, which is love given.)
4. If God is one hypostasis only, then before creation there is no other to love, and God's love in act depends on creation. That contradicts (1).
5. If God is Father, Son and Spirit, eternal love is given and returned within God, with no dependence on creation (1 John 4:8; John 17:24).
6. So unitarian theism must give up (1) or (2). Trinitarian theism keeps both.

**Form:** a disjunctive syllogism over two models of God. It is valid.
**Contested:** (3). The Sunni reply is that the attribute is eternal and only its objects are created. The reply to that: then *al-Wadud* names a capacity, not an act, and an unrealised perfection is not a perfection in act. This contradicts the classical claim that God is pure act without potential.

## Argument 4: the crucifixion as history

1. The crucifixion of Jesus under Pontius Pilate is attested independently by Paul (1 Cor 15:3, c. AD 55), the Gospels, Josephus (*Ant.* 18.63), Tacitus (*Ann.* 15.44) and Mara bar Serapion.
2. No 1st- or 2nd-century source, Christian or hostile, says he was not crucified, apart from Docetists. They denied that he had flesh at all, which is not the Qur'anic view.
3. The Qur'anic denial (Q 4:157) appears about 600 years later, with no earlier source behind it.
4. By ordinary historical method, many early independent witnesses outweigh one late claim.
5. So Q 4:157 is very probably false, and a book with a false historical claim is not wholly God's word.

**Form:** an inductive argument, a probability judgement rather than a proof.
**Contested:** whether revelation overrides historical method. That move works for any claim and so proves nothing. And Argument 1 shows the Qur'an itself appeals to the earlier witnesses.

## Argument 5: the Qur'an's own test of a prophet

1. Deuteronomy 13:1–5 and 18:20–22 (which the Qur'an counts as revealed Torah, Q 5:44) say that a prophet who leads away from the God already revealed is not to be followed, whatever signs he gives.
2. The God revealed in the Torah, Prophets and Gospel is shown (Arguments 1 and 4) to include the crucified and risen Son.
3. Muhammad's message denies (2).
4. So by the test the Qur'an itself endorses, the message fails.

**Form:** modus ponens. It is valid.
**Contested:** (2), which rests on Arguments 1 and 4.

## Summary

| # | Turns Sunni Islam's own claim | Validity | Weakest premise |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Q 5:47, 10:94 against Q 4:157 | valid dilemma | the date of any textual corruption |
| 2 | the uncreated *kalam* | valid reductio | whether an attribute is the same as a hypostasis |
| 3 | *al-Wadud* and *al-Ghani* | valid disjunction | whether love needs an other |
| 4 | Q 4:157 against history | strong induction | whether revelation overrides history |
| 5 | Deut 13/18 via Q 5:44 | valid modus ponens | depends on 1 and 4 |

The arguments add up. Arguments 1, 4 and 5 attack the Qur'an's claim to be revelation. Arguments 2 and 3 remove the main reason Sunni Islam gives for rejecting the Trinity. To answer all five, a Sunni must hold a corruption theory that has no evidence, that a perfection can remain only potential, and that revelation can override all early history. Each is costly, and holding all three together is costlier still.

Sources: St John of Damascus, *On Heresies* 100; Theodore Abu Qurrah; Timothy I's dialogue with al-Mahdi; al-Ghazali's and Ibn Taymiyya's replies (*al-Jawab al-Sahih*) for the Sunni side.
