# Data breach plan

*One page. Last reviewed: November 2026.*

A breach is any loss, theft, wrong sharing or unauthorised access to personal data: a lost phone or laptop, an email to
the wrong parent, a hacked account, a lost exercise book with a child's name on it.

## Who decides
The **owner** ([DSL name]) leads every breach and makes every decision below. A tutor who finds one tells the owner
**the same day**, by phone: [phone].

## The steps
1. **Contain it (first hour).** Recall the email, change passwords, sign the device out of Google, ask a wrong recipient to delete it.
2. **Assess it (first 24 hours).** What data, whose, how many people? Is any about children, health, safeguarding or money? Could it harm anyone?
3. **Decide on the ICO (within 72 hours of finding out).** If there is a risk to people's rights (likely whenever children's,
   health, safeguarding or financial data is involved), report to the ICO within **72 hours**, even before every fact is known:
   ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach, 0303 123 1113. If there is no risk, do not report, but write down why.
4. **Tell the people affected** without delay if the risk is **high**: what happened, what it means, what we are doing,
   what they can do, and who to contact.
5. **Tell others where needed:** Stripe or Google if their service is involved; the police for theft or hacking; the local
   authority (MASH or LADO) if a child could be at risk; our insurer, [insurer].
6. **Learn from it.** Fix the cause within a month and note the change.

## Who is told — at a glance
| Who | When |
|---|---|
| Owner | Same day, always |
| ICO | Within 72 hours, if any risk |
| People affected | Without delay, if high risk |
| Stripe / Google / police / MASH / insurer | When their part applies |
